\begin{abstract}
Let $f(n)$ be the maximum number of stable matchings of a stable-marriage instance with $n$ men
and $n$ women; determining $f$ is a problem of Knuth (1976) and Open Problem~\#1 of Gusfield and
Irving (1989). The value $f(5)=16$ was announced by Eilers (2022, OEIS A357269) on the strength
of an unpublished constraint-programming search that produced no checkable artifact. We give the
first machine-checkable proof. A Lean~4 development defines the problem in $\sim$40 lines,
proves---with no \texttt{sorry} and only the three standard Mathlib axioms---the theorem
\[
\texttt{f5\_eq\_16\_of\_unsat} :\;
\Bigl(\forall\, \rho \in P_{120},\ \neg\mathrm{Sat}(\Phi_\rho)\Bigr) \;\longrightarrow\;
\Bigl(\forall I,\ \mathrm{WF}(I) \to \mathrm{sc}(I) \le 16\Bigr) \wedge
\Bigl(\exists I,\ \mathrm{WF}(I) \wedge \mathrm{sc}(I) = 16\Bigr),
\]
where the $120$ propositional formulas $\Phi_\rho$ are themselves defined in Lean and printed
verbatim to DIMACS by a 30-line exporter. Each $\Phi_\rho$ is refuted by \textsf{kissat}, each
proof is validated by \textsf{drat-trim}, and each resulting LRAT certificate is checked
end-to-end by the formally verified checker \textsf{cake\_lpr}: $120/120$ verified, about two
hours on a laptop. The architecture follows the empty-hexagon development of Subercaseaux et
al.\ (ITP~2024). As by-products we independently confirm Eilers' companion counts (OEIS
A344669): there are exactly $507{,}254{,}400$ maximal profiles of order~$5$
($176{,}130$ up to symmetry), obtained by enumerating all $4{,}227{,}120$ canonical solutions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A stable-marriage instance of order $n$ equips $n$ men and $n$ women each with a strict
preference order over the opposite side; a perfect matching is \emph{stable} if no man and woman
prefer each other to their assigned partners. Gale and Shapley proved stable matchings always
exist. Let $f(n)$ denote the maximum number of stable matchings over instances of order $n$.
Knuth posed the behaviour of $f$ as a research problem in 1976~\cite{knuth}; Gusfield and Irving
made it Open Problem~\#1 of their monograph~\cite{gi89}. Asymptotically
$2.28^n \le f(n) \le 3.55^n$ \cite{thurber,kow,pp}, and exactly (OEIS A357269~\cite{oeis}):
\[
f(1),\dots,f(5) = 1,\,2,\,3,\,10,\,16.
\]
The last and hardest value, $f(5)=16$, was announced by Dan Eilers in September 2022, computed
with a MiniZinc constraint-programming model. The result is trustworthy---we confirm it here by
independent means---but it had, until now, no proof object: constraint solvers do not emit
certificates, the search is not reproducible from any published artifact, and the paper announced
alongside the OEIS entry has not appeared. For contrast, the best human-provable upper bound at
$n=5$ is $3.55^5 \approx 555$.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A machine-checked proof of $f(5)=16$} in the architecture of~\cite{hexagon}:
  a Lean~4 development (zero \texttt{sorry}s; axioms \texttt{propext},
  \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}) reduces the theorem to the unsatisfiability of
  $120$ concrete CNF formulas that are \emph{defined inside Lean} and printed verbatim for the
  solvers; the $120$ refutations are checked by the formally verified proof checker
  \textsf{cake\_lpr}~\cite{cakelpr}. The kernel-checked part comprises a symmetry reduction, a
  case-covering lemma, and the encoding-faithfulness theorem; the lower bound is a
  kernel-computed evaluation of an explicit witness.
\item \textbf{Independent confirmations} of every unreplicated number in this corner of the
  OEIS: $f(5)=16$ itself, and the maximal-profile counts
  A344669$(3,4,5) = 1092,\ 144,\ 507{,}254{,}400$ together with Eilers' reduced count
  $176{,}130$, via exhaustive enumeration with a different toolchain.
\item \textbf{A complete verification artifact}: a repository with a step-by-step third-party
  verification guide (\texttt{VERIFYING.md}), pinned toolchain versions and hashes, a
  differential-testing harness binding the formal definitions to an independently validated
  reference implementation, and continuous integration that rebuilds the development and checks
  the axiom base on every commit.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Lean development}

\paragraph{Definitions.} An instance is a pair of $5\times 5$ rank tables
(\texttt{mrank}$[m][w]$ = position of woman $w$ in man $m$'s order, $0$ best; \texttt{wrank}
symmetric); well-formedness $\mathrm{WF}$ requires both tables to have five rows, each a
permutation of $\{0,\dots,4\}$. A matching is one of the $120$ permutations $\mu$; it is stable
iff no pair $(m,w)$ with $w \ne \mu(m)$ has both
$\mathrm{mrank}[m][w] < \mathrm{mrank}[m][\mu(m)]$ and
$\mathrm{wrank}[w][m] < \mathrm{wrank}[w][\mu^{-1}(w)]$. The count $\mathrm{sc}(I)$ filters
Mathlib's \texttt{List.permutations}. These definitions total about forty lines and are the
entire human-review surface of the formal claim.

\paragraph{Upper bound.} The kernel-checked chain has three stages.
\emph{Symmetry} (\texttt{reduce\_man0'}): relabelling women by man~0's rank row fixes man~0's
row to the identity, preserves well-formedness, and preserves $\mathrm{sc}$; the proof
constructs the relabelling bijection on matchings and carries stability across it pointwise.
\emph{Covering} (\texttt{cube\_covering}): for well-formed $I$, man~1's rank row lies in the
$120$-element list $P_{120} = \texttt{permutations}\,[0,\dots,4]$.
\emph{Faithfulness} (\texttt{cube\_faithful}): for each $\rho \in P_{120}$, any well-formed $I$
with man~0 identity, man~1 row $\rho$, and $\mathrm{sc}(I) \ge 17$ yields an assignment
satisfying the CNF $\Phi_\rho$ of Section~\ref{sec:cnf} --- the assignment reads the preference
variables off $I$ and points seventeen selector slots at the first seventeen stable matchings of
$I$ in enumeration order; each clause family is then discharged. Chaining the three: if every
$\Phi_\rho$ is unsatisfiable then $\mathrm{sc}(I) \le 16$ for all well-formed $I$.

\paragraph{Lower bound.} An explicit witness instance (found by SAT in $0.1$s) is embedded, and
$\mathrm{sc}(\texttt{witnessI}) = 16$ is proved by kernel computation. Mathlib's
\texttt{permutations} uses well-founded recursion, which the kernel cannot evaluate, so the
count is transported across \texttt{List.permutations\_perm\_permutations\('\)} to the
structurally recursive \texttt{permutations\('\)} and evaluated there by \texttt{decide}. A
standalone variant (\texttt{Witness.lean}) proves the same count from first principles using
only \texttt{propext}.

\paragraph{Main theorem.} \texttt{f5\_eq\_16\_of\_unsat} (statement in the abstract) combines
both directions; \texttt{\#print axioms} reports exactly
\texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}, re-checked by CI on every commit.

\section{The certificates}\label{sec:cnf}

\paragraph{Encoding.} $\Phi_\rho$ uses pairwise preference variables with transitivity
(forbidding both $3$-cycles on every triple), unit clauses pinning man~0 to the identity and
man~1 to $\rho$, and a \emph{selector} block: variables $y_{t,\mu}$ ($t < 17$, $\mu$ indexing
$P_{120}$) with (i) each slot nonempty, (ii) consecutive slots strictly increasing in
$\mu$-index (killing slot symmetry and forcing seventeen distinct selections), and (iii)
selected matchings admitting no blocking pair. Sizes: $2{,}140$ variables, $157{,}197$ clauses.
The encoding was chosen to make faithfulness formalizable by hand; no cardinality-network
semantics enter the proof. As a positive control, the $16$-slot variant of the cube containing
the witness is satisfiable, and the decoded model recounts to a valid $16$-matching instance.

\paragraph{Pipeline.} The formulas are printed from the Lean definitions by a $30$-line
exporter. Each cube is refuted by \textsf{kissat}~4.0.4 (hardest cube $115$s --- the one
containing the extremal instances), each DRAT proof is validated and elaborated to LRAT by
\textsf{drat-trim}, and each LRAT is checked by \textsf{cake\_lpr}, whose soundness is itself
machine-checked down to machine code~\cite{cakelpr}. Result: \textbf{120/120
\texttt{s VERIFIED UNSAT}}, about two hours end-to-end on an Apple M4~Max (8-way parallel).
Outside the Lean kernel the trust base is \textsf{cake\_lpr} and the $30$-line printer, and
nothing else --- the solvers are checked, not trusted.

\section{Cross-validation}

Four independent anchors tie the formal definitions to reality:
(1) a reference counter validated against the extremal Latin instances of orders $3,4,5,6$
recorded in OEIS A351413 ($3/10/9/48$, all reproduced);
(2) a differential test: on $300$ random instances the Lean definitions and the reference
counter agree exactly;
(3) the witness chain: SAT model $\to$ reference recount $\to$ Lean kernel count, all $16$;
(4) enumeration: blocking-clause enumeration over all $120$ cubes yields exactly
$4{,}227{,}120$ canonical (man-$0$ = id) profiles with $16$ stable matchings; multiplying by the
$5!$ woman-relabellings gives $507{,}254{,}400 = $ A344669$(5)$, and dividing by the $4!$
man-relabellings gives $176{,}130$, Eilers' reduced count --- both previously unreplicated. The
same runs confirm A344669$(3) = 1092$ (full brute force over all $46{,}656$ profiles) and
A344669$(4) = 144$ (six canonical solutions).

Before adopting the present architecture we also refuted ``$\ge 17$'' monolithically three
times (different encodings and solver configurations, including a pure-RUP
\textsf{kissat --plain} proof of $2.1$GB verified by \textsf{drat-trim}, and a Mathlib
\texttt{lrat\_proof} kernel import of the analogous $n{=}4$ certificate, which reproves
$f(4)=10$ inside Lean in ten seconds).

\section{Related work}

Our architecture --- verified encoding in Lean, certificates checked by \textsf{cake\_lpr},
unsatisfiability entering the main theorem as an explicit hypothesis --- is that of the empty
hexagon number verification~\cite{hexagon}. The end-to-end Keller-conjecture
verification~\cite{keller} has since internalized even the checker into Lean by reflection;
migrating our certificate step onto such a checker (or LRAT-Catcher~\cite{lratcatcher}) is
mechanical future work. Other certified computational values include $R(4,5)=25$~\cite{r45},
Schur number five, the packing chromatic number of the square grid, and
$K_8(4,2)=23$~\cite{k8}. On the stable-matching side the computational lineage is Eilers'
($f(4)$, $f(5)$, the profile counts) building on Thurber~\cite{thurber} and ultimately
Knuth~\cite{knuth}; the asymptotic bounds are \cite{kow,pp}.

\section{Conclusion}

$f(5)=16$ now has a proof whose every step is checked either by the Lean kernel or by a
formally verified certificate checker, with a forty-line human-review surface. The first open
case is $f(6)$: the best lower bound is $48$ (a dihedral Latin instance), conjectured exact
(OEIS A357271). The search space grows by eleven orders of magnitude, so a direct extension
will require either much stronger symmetry breaking inside the certificates or a structural
route through rotation posets --- for which the $176{,}130$ extremal instances enumerated here
are the natural microscope.

\paragraph{Artifact.} Repository with code, proofs, logs, verification guide
(\texttt{VERIFYING.md}) and CI; certificate archive with DOI to accompany the final version.
Reproduction cost: $\sim$20 minutes for the Lean development, $\sim$2--3 hours for the
certificates.
