\begin{abstract}
Let $f(n)$ be the maximum number of stable matchings of a stable-marriage instance with $n$ men
and $n$ women; determining $f$ is a problem of Knuth (1976) and Open Problem~\#1 of Gusfield and
Irving (1989). The value $f(5)=16$ was announced by Eilers (2022, OEIS A357269) on the strength
of an unpublished constraint-programming search that produced no checkable artifact. We give the
first machine-checkable proof. A Lean~4 development defines the problem in $\sim$40 lines,
proves---with no \texttt{sorry} and only the three standard Mathlib axioms---the theorem
\begin{align*}
\texttt{f5\_eq\_16\_of\_unsat} :\;
\Bigl(\forall\, \rho \in P_{120},\ \neg\mathrm{Sat}(\Phi_\rho)\Bigr) \;\longrightarrow\;
&\Bigl(\forall I,\ \mathrm{WF}(I) \to \mathrm{sc}(I) \le 16\Bigr)\\
&{}\wedge \Bigl(\exists I,\ \mathrm{WF}(I) \wedge \mathrm{sc}(I) = 16\Bigr),
\end{align*}
where the $120$ propositional formulas $\Phi_\rho$ are themselves defined in Lean and printed
verbatim to DIMACS by a 30-line exporter. Each $\Phi_\rho$ is refuted by \textsf{kissat}, each
proof is validated by \textsf{drat-trim}, and each resulting LRAT certificate is checked
end-to-end by the formally verified checker \textsf{cake\_lpr}: $120/120$ verified, about two
hours on a laptop. The architecture follows the empty-hexagon development of Subercaseaux et
al.\ (ITP~2024). As by-products we independently confirm Eilers' companion counts (OEIS
A344669): there are exactly $507{,}254{,}400$ maximal profiles of order~$5$
($176{,}130$ up to symmetry), obtained by enumerating all $4{,}227{,}120$ canonical solutions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A stable-marriage instance of order $n$ equips $n$ men and $n$ women each with a strict
preference order over the opposite side; a perfect matching is \emph{stable} if no man and woman
prefer each other to their assigned partners. Gale and Shapley proved stable matchings always
exist. Let $f(n)$ denote the maximum number of stable matchings over instances of order $n$.
Knuth posed the behaviour of $f$ as a research problem in 1976~\cite{knuth}; Gusfield and Irving
made it Open Problem~\#1 of their monograph~\cite{gi89}. Asymptotically
$2.28^n \le f(n) \le 3.55^n$ \cite{thurber,kow,pp}, and exactly (OEIS A357269~\cite{oeis}):
\[
f(1),\dots,f(5) = 1,\,2,\,3,\,10,\,16.
\]
The last and hardest value, $f(5)=16$, was announced by Dan Eilers in September 2022, computed
with a MiniZinc constraint-programming model. The result is trustworthy---we confirm it here by
independent means---but it had, until now, no proof object: constraint solvers do not emit
certificates, the search is not reproducible from any published artifact, and the paper announced
alongside the OEIS entry has not appeared. For contrast, the best human-provable upper bound at
$n=5$ is $3.55^5 \approx 555$.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A machine-checked proof of $f(5)=16$} in the architecture of~\cite{hexagon}:
  a Lean~4 development (zero \texttt{sorry}s; axioms \texttt{propext},
  \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}) reduces the theorem to the unsatisfiability of
  $120$ concrete CNF formulas that are \emph{defined inside Lean} and printed verbatim for the
  solvers; the $120$ refutations are checked by the formally verified proof checker
  \textsf{cake\_lpr}~\cite{cakelpr}. The kernel-checked part comprises a symmetry reduction, a
  case-covering lemma, and the encoding-faithfulness theorem; the lower bound is a
  kernel-computed evaluation of an explicit witness.
\item \textbf{Independent confirmations} of every unreplicated number in this corner of the
  OEIS: $f(5)=16$ itself, and the maximal-profile counts
  A344669$(3,4,5) = 1092,\ 144,\ 507{,}254{,}400$ together with Eilers' reduced count
  $176{,}130$, via exhaustive enumeration with a different toolchain.
\item \textbf{A complete verification artifact}: a repository with a step-by-step third-party
  verification guide (\texttt{VERIFYING.md}), pinned toolchain versions and hashes, a
  differential-testing harness binding the formal definitions to an independently validated
  reference implementation, and continuous integration that rebuilds the development and checks
  the axiom base on every commit.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Lean development}

\subsection{The review surface}

The claim of a formal theorem is only as strong as the definitions in its statement, so we
display them in full. An instance is a pair of $5\times 5$ rank tables; everything is built
from \texttt{List Nat}:

\begin{verbatim}
structure Inst where
  mrank : List (List Nat)   -- mrank[m][w] = position of w in m's order, 0 best
  wrank : List (List Nat)   -- symmetric

def isRankRow (r : List Nat) : Bool :=
  r.length = 5 && (List.range 5).all fun v => r.contains v

def WF (I : Inst) : Bool :=
  I.mrank.length = 5 && I.wrank.length = 5 &&
  I.mrank.all isRankRow && I.wrank.all isRankRow

def get2 (t : List (List Nat)) (i j : Nat) : Nat := (t.getD i []).getD j 0

def isStable (I : Inst) (mu : List Nat) : Bool :=
  (List.range 5).all fun m => (List.range 5).all fun w =>
    let wm := mu.getD m 0        -- m's partner
    let mw := idxOf w mu         -- w's partner
    (w = wm) || !(get2 I.mrank m w < get2 I.mrank m wm &&
                  get2 I.wrank w m < get2 I.wrank w mw)

def stableCount (I : Inst) : Nat :=
  (([0,1,2,3,4] : List Nat).permutations.filter (isStable I)).length
\end{verbatim}

\noindent (plus the seven-line first-order recursion \texttt{idxOf}). This, together with
the statement below, is the entire human-review surface; nothing else needs to be read to
trust the theorem:

\begin{verbatim}
theorem f5_eq_16_of_unsat
    (H : forall row in perms120, not (Satisfiable (cubeCNF row))) :
    (forall I : Inst, WF I = true -> stableCount I <= 16) /\
    (exists I : Inst, WF I = true /\ stableCount I = 16)
\end{verbatim}

\noindent where \texttt{perms120} and \texttt{cubeCNF} are likewise concrete Lean
definitions (Section~\ref{sec:cnf}), so the single hypothesis \texttt{H} is exactly what
the verified certificate checker discharges.

\subsection{Upper bound: symmetry, covering, faithfulness}

The kernel-checked chain has three stages.

\emph{Symmetry} (\texttt{reduce\_man0'}, \texttt{Symmetry.lean}, $416$ lines): relabelling
women by man~0's rank row $\sigma$ fixes man~0's row to the identity while preserving
well-formedness and $\mathrm{sc}$. The proof constructs the relabelling explicitly ---
\texttt{relabel} composes every man's row with $\sigma^{-1}$ and permutes the women's rows
--- together with the induced bijection $\mu \mapsto \sigma^{-1}\circ\mu$ on matchings, and
carries stability across it pointwise. The workhorses are four small lemmas about the
first-order \texttt{idxOf} recursion (\texttt{idxOf\_lt\_length}, \texttt{getD\_idxOf},
\texttt{idxOf\_getD}, \texttt{idxOf\_map}) which together say that on lists that are
permutations of $[0,\dots,4]$, indexing and inversion behave like the group theory one
would do on paper. No \texttt{Equiv.Perm} or finite-group machinery is used; staying
first-order over lists keeps the definitions kernel-computable and the faithfulness proofs
syntactic.

\emph{Covering} (\texttt{cube\_covering}): for well-formed $I$, man~1's rank row lies in
the $120$-element list $P_{120} = \texttt{permutations}\,[0,\dots,4]$; this is
\texttt{rankRow\_perm} plus membership transport, and it is what makes the $120$ cubes
exhaustive by construction rather than by convention.

\emph{Faithfulness} (\texttt{cube\_faithful'}, \texttt{Faithfulness.lean}, $429$ lines):
for each $\rho \in P_{120}$, any well-formed $I$ with man~0 identity, man~1 row $\rho$, and
$\mathrm{sc}(I) \ge 17$ yields an assignment satisfying the CNF $\Phi_\rho$ of
Section~\ref{sec:cnf}. Chaining the three: if every $\Phi_\rho$ is unsatisfiable then
$\mathrm{sc}(I) \le 16$ for all well-formed $I$.

\subsection{Lower bound: a kernel-computed witness}

An explicit witness instance (found by SAT in $0.1$s) is embedded as a literal:
\begin{verbatim}
def witnessI : Inst :=
  ⟨[[2,0,1,4,3], [1,3,4,2,0], [2,4,3,0,1], [4,1,0,2,3], [0,2,3,4,1]],
   [[1,3,2,0,4], [4,1,0,3,2], [2,0,1,4,3], [1,2,3,4,0], [0,4,3,1,2]]⟩
\end{verbatim}
and $\mathrm{sc}(\texttt{witnessI}) = 16$ is proved by kernel computation. One subtlety is
worth recording for practitioners: Mathlib's \texttt{List.permutations} is defined by
well-founded recursion, which the kernel cannot unfold, so \texttt{decide} fails on
\texttt{stableCount} as stated. The count is transported across the Mathlib equivalence
\texttt{List.permutations\_perm\_permutations\('\)} to the structurally recursive
\texttt{permutations\('\)} and evaluated there --- a two-line fix once diagnosed, invisible
in the final statement. A standalone variant (\texttt{Witness.lean}) proves the same count
from first principles using only \texttt{propext}. We deliberately avoid
\texttt{native\_decide} throughout: every computation in the trust base is kernel-reduced.

\paragraph{Main theorem.} \texttt{f5\_eq\_16\_of\_unsat} combines both directions;
\texttt{\#print axioms} reports exactly \texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]},
re-checked --- together with a no-\texttt{sorry} sweep and a rebuild from a clean cache ---
by continuous integration on every commit.

\section{The certificates}\label{sec:cnf}

\paragraph{Encoding.} The variables of $\Phi_\rho$ come in two blocks. \emph{Preference
variables}: for each side $s\in\{\text{men},\text{women}\}$, person $i<5$ and pair
$a<b<5$, a variable $p^s_{i,a,b}$ meaning ``person $i$ prefers $a$ to $b$''
($2\cdot5\cdot10=100$ variables); a literal for ``prefers $a$ to $b$'' with $a>b$ is the
negation. \emph{Selector variables}: $y_{t,\mu}$ for $t<17$ and $\mu$ ranging over the
concrete list $P_{120}$ of matchings ($17\cdot120=2040$ variables; $2140$ in all). The
clause families:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{transitivity}: for every person and ordered triple, forbid both preference
  $3$-cycles ($2\cdot 5\cdot\binom{5}{3}\cdot 2$ clauses) --- with totality this makes each
  person's preference relation a strict linear order;
\item \emph{cube units}: unit clauses pinning man~0's row to the identity and man~1's row
  to $\rho$;
\item \emph{slot nonemptiness}: $\bigvee_\mu y_{t,\mu}$ for each $t<17$;
\item \emph{slot ordering}: $\neg y_{t,\mu} \vee \neg y_{t+1,\mu'}$ for $\mu' \le \mu$ in
  the fixed enumeration order of $P_{120}$ --- consecutive slots strictly increase, killing
  the $17!$ slot symmetry and forcing seventeen \emph{distinct} selections;
\item \emph{non-blocking}: $\neg y_{t,\mu} \vee \neg p^m_{m,w,\mu(m)} \vee
  \neg p^w_{w,m,\mu^{-1}(w)}$ for every slot, matching and non-matched pair $(m,w)$ ---
  a selected matching admits no blocking pair.
\end{itemize}
Together: $157{,}197$ clauses. The encoding is deliberately \emph{naive}: no cardinality
networks, no auxiliary Tseitin variables, because every clause family must be discharged by
hand in the faithfulness proof. Satisfiability of $\Phi_\rho$ says precisely ``some
instance in cube $\rho$ has $17$ pairwise-distinct stable matchings''.

\paragraph{Faithfulness, in detail.} Given well-formed $I$ in cube $\rho$ with
$\mathrm{sc}(I)\ge 17$, the assignment $\tau$ sets $p^s_{i,a,b}$ by comparing the actual
ranks in $I$, and sets $y_{t,\mu}$ true iff $\mu$ is the $t$-th stable matching of $I$ in
the enumeration order of $P_{120}$ (the first seventeen indices exist because
$\mathrm{sc}(I)\ge 17$). Six lemmas then discharge the six families
(\texttt{trans\_sat}, \texttt{man0\_sat}, \texttt{man1\_sat}, \texttt{nonempty\_sat},
\texttt{ordering\_sat}, \texttt{block\_sat}); the last is the delicate one, unfolding the
Boolean \texttt{isStable} of the selected matching at the offending pair. The composition
is \texttt{cube\_faithful'}. As a positive control against vacuity, the $16$-slot variant
of the cube containing the witness is satisfiable, and the decoded model recounts to a
valid $16$-matching instance.

\paragraph{Pipeline.} The formulas are printed from the Lean definitions by a $30$-line
exporter. Each cube is refuted by \textsf{kissat}~4.0.4 (hardest cube $115$s --- the one
containing the extremal instances), each DRAT proof is validated and elaborated to LRAT by
\textsf{drat-trim}, and each LRAT is checked by \textsf{cake\_lpr}, whose soundness is itself
machine-checked down to machine code~\cite{cakelpr}. Result: \textbf{120/120
\texttt{s VERIFIED UNSAT}}, about two hours end-to-end on an Apple M4~Max (8-way parallel).
Outside the Lean kernel the trust base is \textsf{cake\_lpr} and the $30$-line printer, and
nothing else --- the solvers are checked, not trusted.

\section{Cross-validation}

Four independent anchors tie the formal definitions to reality:
(1) a reference counter validated against the extremal Latin instances of orders $3,4,5,6$
recorded in OEIS A351413 ($3/10/9/48$, all reproduced);
(2) a differential test: on $300$ random instances the Lean definitions and the reference
counter agree exactly;
(3) the witness chain: SAT model $\to$ reference recount $\to$ Lean kernel count, all $16$;
(4) enumeration: blocking-clause enumeration over all $120$ cubes yields exactly
$4{,}227{,}120$ canonical (man-$0$ = id) profiles with $16$ stable matchings; multiplying by the
$5!$ woman-relabellings gives $507{,}254{,}400 = $ A344669$(5)$, and dividing by the $4!$
man-relabellings gives $176{,}130$, Eilers' reduced count --- both previously unreplicated. The
same runs confirm A344669$(3) = 1092$ (full brute force over all $46{,}656$ profiles) and
A344669$(4) = 144$ (six canonical solutions).

Before adopting the present architecture we also refuted ``$\ge 17$'' monolithically three
times (different encodings and solver configurations, including a pure-RUP
\textsf{kissat --plain} proof of $2.1$GB verified by \textsf{drat-trim}, and a Mathlib
\texttt{lrat\_proof} kernel import of the analogous $n{=}4$ certificate, which reproves
$f(4)=10$ inside Lean in ten seconds).

\noindent The development builds in about twenty minutes (Mathlib cached); the kernel
evaluation of the witness count takes seconds. Some transferable lessons:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Single source of truth for the encoding.} The classic gap in certified-SAT
  papers is between the CNF the paper describes and the CNF the solver saw. Here the CNF
  is a Lean value and the DIMACS files are printed from it by a $30$-line exporter, so the
  faithfulness theorem is about the very formulas that were refuted. The exporter is in
  the trust base; it is small enough to read in one sitting.
\item \emph{Stay first-order, stay computable.} All objects are lists of naturals;
  stability is a \texttt{Bool}-valued program. This makes every quantity in the
  development kernel-computable (\texttt{decide} works), keeps proofs syntactic, and
  avoids simultaneously fighting the mathlib abstraction stack and the SAT bridge.
\item \emph{Certified results as test oracles.} Before formalization, the counting
  function was validated against OEIS ground truth and by differential testing; after
  formalization, the certified theorem became the oracle for the (unverified) $f(6)$
  search machinery of the companion paper --- certification pays forward.
\item \emph{Positive controls.} Every UNSAT campaign ran alongside a SAT control (the
  $16$-slot cube of the witness) to guard against vacuous refutations of over-constrained
  encodings --- the SAT analogue of testing that your test suite can fail.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{On AI assistance.} The development --- problem selection, Lean proofs,
encodings, search code, and this paper --- was produced by the author working with
Claude (Anthropic) in an agentic coding setup, over roughly three days. The formal
artifact is unaffected in kind by this provenance: the theorem statement is forty lines of
displayed Lean, the axioms are printed by the kernel, and the certificates are checked by
a verified checker. We note it because the workflow --- an AI system iterating against a
proof assistant, with the human directing scope, priorities and publication decisions ---
was essential to the three-day cost, and because problem-selection due diligence (checking
OEIS entries, problem pages and prior announcements) consumed a substantial fraction of
the effort, which we suspect generalizes.

\section{Related work}

Our architecture --- verified encoding in Lean, certificates checked by \textsf{cake\_lpr},
unsatisfiability entering the main theorem as an explicit hypothesis --- is that of the empty
hexagon number verification~\cite{hexagon}. The end-to-end Keller-conjecture
verification~\cite{keller} has since internalized even the checker into Lean by reflection;
migrating our certificate step onto such a checker (or LRAT-Catcher~\cite{lratcatcher}) is
mechanical future work. Other certified computational values include $R(4,5)=25$~\cite{r45},
Schur number five, the packing chromatic number of the square grid, and
$K_8(4,2)=23$~\cite{k8}. On the stable-matching side the computational lineage is Eilers'
($f(4)$, $f(5)$, the profile counts) building on Thurber~\cite{thurber} and ultimately
Knuth~\cite{knuth}; the asymptotic bounds are \cite{kow,pp}.

\section{Conclusion}

$f(5)=16$ now has a proof whose every step is checked either by the Lean kernel or by a
formally verified certificate checker, with a forty-line human-review surface. The companion
paper~\cite{f6paper} takes the next step: it determines the previously open value $f(6)=48$
by reducing instance space ($\sim\!10^{28}$) to an exhaustively enumerated space of rotation
schedules ($2.7\times 10^{10}$), with the certified theorem proved here serving as ground
truth for validating that enumeration machinery. The reduction lemmas of~\cite{f6paper} are
themselves formalized in Lean (a further $1{,}849$ lines, $105$ theorems, zero
\texttt{sorry}s, in the same repository); certifying its enumeration end-to-end is the
natural continuation of the programme begun here.

\paragraph{Artifact.} Repository with code, proofs, logs, verification guide
(\texttt{VERIFYING.md}) and CI; certificate archive with DOI to accompany the final version.
Reproduction cost: $\sim$20 minutes for the Lean development, $\sim$2--3 hours for the
certificates.
