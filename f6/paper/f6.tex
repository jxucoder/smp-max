\begin{abstract}
Let $f(n)$ denote the maximum number of stable matchings of a stable-marriage instance with $n$ men
and $n$ women (Knuth 1976; Gusfield--Irving Open Problem \#1). The known values were
$f(1),\dots,f(5)=1,2,3,10,16$; the value of $f(6)$ was open, with the lower bound $48$ (a dihedral
Latin instance) conjectured exact (OEIS A357271). We establish $f(6)=48$. The proof is a reduction
of the $\sim\!10^{28}$-instance search space to a finite space of \emph{rotation schedules}:
sequences of cyclic partner exchanges applied from the identity matching. Three elementary lemmas
over the standard rotation theory show that every instance is dominated, in stable-matching count,
by the \emph{read-off instance} of its own schedule, and that a first-appearance labeling loses no
schedules; the validity and bridge lemmas are machine-checked in Lean~4 (no \texttt{sorry},
standard axioms). The upper bound is then discharged by SAT certificates over a formula defined in
Lean, asserting that a legal schedule reads off to at least $49$ stable matchings: the canonical
schedules are covered by $25{,}493$ root cubes, refined by the solver's splits into $321{,}492$
cubes, whose $318{,}736$ leaves were all refuted by CaDiCaL with every refutation checked by the
formally verified LRAT checker cake\_lpr ($0$ satisfiable; the journal is audited). The base
formula, the cube lists, the certified cube set and every cube formula were re-derived from the
Lean definitions and match what the solver saw. The Lean theorem that ties the formula to the
count --- every well-formed instance with at least $49$ stable matchings satisfies some certified
cube's formula --- is the remaining link; its status is recorded in the repository's
\texttt{STATUS.md} at the commit cited in Section~\ref{sec:outlook}.

\section{Introduction}

A stable-marriage instance of order $n$ equips $n$ men and $n$ women each with a strict preference
order over the opposite side; a perfect matching is \emph{stable} if no man and woman prefer each
other to their assigned partners. Knuth~\cite{knuth} asked for the behaviour of $f(n)$, the maximum
number of stable matchings over instances of order $n$; Gusfield and Irving made it Open
Problem~\#1 of their monograph~\cite{gi89}. Asymptotically $2.28^n\le f(n)\le 3.55^n$
\cite{thurber,kow,pp}. Exact values have been scarce: $f(4)=10$ is classical (Eilers'
uniqueness observation) and $f(5)=16$ is due to Eilers~\cite{oeis} (obtained in 2022 by constraint
programming and certified formally in the author's companion work~\cite{f5paper}), and
\[
f(1),\dots,f(5) \;=\; 1,\;2,\;3,\;10,\;16.
\]
For $n=6$ the best lower bound has long been $48$, attained by a Latin instance built from the
dihedral group; OEIS A357271 records the conjecture that $48$ is exact. The instance space of
order six ($\sim\!10^{28}$ after obvious symmetries) is far beyond direct search, and the best
human-provable upper bound, $3.55^6\approx 2005$, is forty times too large.

\paragraph{Result and method.} We prove $f(6)=48$. The key is a change of universe: instead of
instances, we enumerate \emph{schedules} --- sequences of cyclic partner exchanges ("rotations")
applied from the identity matching, subject to the budget constraints that rotation theory imposes
(at most $30$ moved-man slots, at most $5$ moves per man, no partner revisited). Each schedule
\emph{reads off} an instance whose preference lists are the participants' partner trajectories,
completed at the bottom. Three lemmas (Section~\ref{sec:reduction}) show:
(i) every instance's rotations, in elimination order, form a valid schedule;
(ii) the read-off instance of that schedule has at least as many stable matchings
(the \emph{bridge lemma}); and
(iii) a first-appearance labeling rule preserves at least one representative of every schedule
without affecting read-off counts (a commutation normal form, used by the enumeration only, does
likewise).
The schedule space is small enough to certify: the canonical schedules are covered by $25{,}493$
root cubes of a Lean-defined propositional formula asserting a read-off count of at least $49$;
refined by the solver's splits into $321{,}492$ cubes, all $318{,}736$ leaves are refuted, each
with a certificate checked by the verified checker cake\_lpr (Section~\ref{sec:campaign}). With the
dihedral lower bound, $f(6)=48$ follows. An exhaustive enumeration that visits
$26{,}574{,}282{,}886$ schedule nodes and computes the exact stable-matching count of every node's
read-off instance, attaining maximum $48$ (Section~\ref{sec:enum}), corroborates the certificates
by a method sharing no code with them.

\paragraph{Validation.} Because the upper bound rests on computation, Section~\ref{sec:campaign}
records the audit of the certificate journal and the identity checks between the Lean-defined
formula and cube lists and what the solver saw, and Section~\ref{sec:valid} documents the
corroborating checks: the enumerator run at orders $3,4,5$ reproduces $f=3,10,16$ ---
the last value being formally certified (machine-checked in Lean, with SAT refutations verified by
the formally verified checker cake\_lpr) in the companion work~\cite{f5paper}, so a certified
theorem serves as test oracle for the present method; an independently written enumerator agrees
on maxima; disabling the symmetry reductions multiplies the tree by up to $48\times$ (a
$2.66\times10^9$-node check at order six) and never changes a maximum; and $1.3\times10^9$
local-search evaluations in two other search spaces also never exceeded $48$.
Section~\ref{sec:lean} describes the Lean~4 formalization of the reduction and states which links
of the proof are Lean theorems.

\section{Preliminaries}

We use the structure theory of the stable-matching lattice and its rotations
(Gusfield--Irving~\cite{gi89}, Chapters 2--3) and record what we need.

\begin{fact}\label{fact:pairs}
Call $(m,w)$ a \emph{stable pair} of an instance $I$ if some stable matching contains it. For each
man $m$, his stable partners ordered by his preference form his \emph{trajectory} $T_m$: the
man-optimal matching $\mu_0$ gives each man the first entry of his trajectory, and the rotations of
$I$, applied from $\mu_0$ in any linear extension of the rotation poset, move each man strictly
down his trajectory, one entry per rotation containing him, ending at the woman-optimal matching.
Symmetrically each woman moves strictly up her trajectory. A rotation is a cyclic sequence
$(m_0,w_0),\dots,(m_{k-1},w_{k-1})$, $k\ge 2$, of pairs of the current matching, whose application
gives $m_i$ the partner $w_{i+1}$ of $m_{i+1}$ (indices mod $k$). The number of stable matchings
equals the number of downsets of the rotation poset.
\end{fact}

\begin{fact}\label{fact:budget}
Each man is moved by exactly $|T_m|-1\le n-1$ rotations, so the total move count satisfies
$\sum_\rho |\rho| = \sum_m (|T_m|-1)\le n(n-1)$; for $n=6$: at most $30$ slots, at most $5$ moves
per man, and every rotation moves at least two men.
\end{fact}

\section{The schedule reduction}\label{sec:reduction}

\begin{definition}[Schedule]\label{def:sched}
Fix $n=6$ and start from the identity matching. A \emph{schedule} is a sequence of \emph{steps},
each a cyclic move on $k\ge 2$ men (man $m_i$ takes the current wife of $m_{i+1}$), subject to:
total move count $\le 30$, at most $5$ moves per man, and no man ever moving to a wife he has
already had, nor any woman receiving a husband she has already had.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Read-off instance]\label{def:readoff}
Given a schedule $S$, the read-off instance $R(S)$ ranks, for each man, his trajectory in order at
the top of his list, followed by the remaining women in a fixed canonical order; for each woman,
her trajectory \emph{reversed} at the top, followed by the remaining men canonically.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Validity]\label{lem:valid}
For any instance $I$ of order $6$, relabel women so that the man-optimal matching is the identity.
Then the rotations of $I$, in any linear extension of the rotation poset, form a schedule $S(I)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Rotations are cyclic moves on $k\ge 2$ men exposed in the current matching, and applying them in a
linear extension realizes the lattice traversal, in which men move strictly down and women strictly
up their trajectories (hence no repeats). The caps are Fact~\ref{fact:budget}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bridge]\label{lem:bridge}
For every instance $I$ of order $6$ (women relabelled as above),
$\mathrm{sc}(I)\le\mathrm{sc}(R(S(I)))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $J=R(S(I))$. The trajectories of $S(I)$ are the stable-partner sequences of $I$
(Fact~\ref{fact:pairs}); thus for each man, $J$ ranks his $I$-stable partners in the same relative
order as $I$ does, with all other women strictly below, and symmetrically for women. We claim
every $I$-stable matching $\mu$ is $J$-stable, which proves the inequality. Every pair of $\mu$ is
a stable pair, hence a trajectory pair on both sides. Let $(m,w)$, $w\ne\mu(m)$.
If $w\notin T_m$: in $J$, $m$ ranks $w$ below all of $T_m$, in particular below $\mu(m)$; no block.
If $w\in T_m$: then $(m,w)$ is a stable pair, so $m\in T_w$; also $\mu(m)\in T_m$ and
$\mu^{-1}(w)\in T_w$. Since $J$ preserves relative $I$-order within trajectories on both sides,
$(m,w)$ blocks $\mu$ in $J$ iff it blocks in $I$ --- and it does not.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Symmetry soundness]\label{lem:sym}
(i) Exchanging two adjacent steps with disjoint man-sets leaves all trajectories, hence $R(S)$,
unchanged. (ii) Relabelling men and women by a common permutation $\sigma$ maps legal schedules to
legal schedules and preserves the stable-matching count of the read-off:
$\mathrm{sc}(R(\sigma\cdot S))=\mathrm{sc}(R(S))$. Consequently an enumeration that
(a) requires the new men of each step to be exactly the next unused labels as a set, and
(b) rejects a step that is smaller, in a fixed total order on step shapes, than an earlier step
separated from it only by man-disjoint steps,\footnote{Any total order on step shapes is sound.
\texttt{gen\_enum.c} uses the index order of its size-first shape table; the certificate campaign
of Section~\ref{sec:campaign} uses rule (a) only.}
still attains $\max_S \mathrm{sc}(R(S))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Steps with disjoint man-sets touch disjoint women (their women are their men's current wives).
(ii) Legality (budget, per-man cap, no revisit) is a property of the trajectories and is invariant
under relabelling both sides by $\sigma$. The count equality is \emph{not} an identity of
instances: $R(\sigma\cdot S)\ne\sigma(R(S))$ in general, because the bottom completion of
Definition~\ref{def:readoff} uses the fixed ascending order. Instead: in $R(S)$ the identity is
man-optimal and the final matching woman-optimal, and every intermediate matching is stable (a pair
$(m,w)$ with $m$ having held $w$ earlier cannot block, since $w$ would have to hold $m$ again
later), so each person's stable partners in $R(S)$ are exactly their trajectory entries, in
trajectory order. $R(\sigma\cdot S)$ ranks precisely these partners of $\sigma(R(S))$ on top in the
same order, so the bottom-agnostic bridge of Section~\ref{sec:lean}
(\texttt{count\_le\_of\_orderPreserving}), instantiated at the relabelled instance
$\sigma(R(S))$ and the relabelled schedule $\sigma\cdot S$, gives
$\mathrm{sc}(R(S))=\mathrm{sc}(\sigma(R(S)))\le\mathrm{sc}(R(\sigma\cdot S))$; applying
$\sigma^{-1}$ to $\sigma\cdot S$ gives the reverse inequality. This re-instantiation is how the
Lean development proves the inequality it uses (at the chain schedule of an instance).
For the consequence, relabel by first participation (any assignment within a step's new set
satisfies the set rule), and note rule (b) discards a sequence only in favour of a
commutation-equivalent one with the same read-off instance.
\end{proof}


\section{A worked example}\label{sec:example}

Order $3$ is small enough to display the entire reduction. Consider the cyclic Latin
instance $I_3$ (each row lists partners from most to least preferred):
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
m_0 & w_0 & w_1 & w_2\\
m_1 & w_2 & w_0 & w_1\\
m_2 & w_1 & w_2 & w_0
\end{array}
\qquad\qquad
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
w_0 & m_2 & m_1 & m_0\\
w_1 & m_1 & m_0 & m_2\\
w_2 & m_0 & m_2 & m_1
\end{array}
\]
It has $f(3)=3$ stable matchings. In the man-optimal one every man gets his first
choice: $\mu_0=\{m_0w_0,\,m_1w_2,\,m_2w_1\}$; the woman-optimal one gives every woman
hers. The rotation poset is a $2$-chain of two rotations, each moving all three men, so
the number of downsets is $3$, as it must be.

Relabel women so that $\mu_0$ becomes the identity ($w_0\mapsto 0$, $w_2\mapsto 1$,
$w_1\mapsto 2$). The two rotations become two applications of the same cyclic step
$(m_0\,m_2\,m_1)$ --- written $(021)$ in min-first form --- taking the matching through
\[
(0,1,2)\ \longrightarrow\ (2,0,1)\ \longrightarrow\ (1,2,0).
\]
Man $m_0$'s trajectory is $(w_0,w_2,w_1)$, which is exactly his (relabelled) preference
order: at order $3$ every pair of $I_3$ is a stable pair, so the trajectories exhaust the
preference lists, the bottom completion is empty, and the read-off instance
$R(S(I_3))$ \emph{is} $I_3$ itself --- the instance is a fixed point of the reduction,
as every Latin extremal instance is.

The entire order-3 schedule space, enumerated under Definition~\ref{def:sched} with the
reductions of Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}, has eight nodes:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc@{\qquad\qquad}lc}
\toprule
schedule & $\mathrm{sc}(R(S))$ & schedule & $\mathrm{sc}(R(S))$\\
\midrule
$\varepsilon$    & 1 & $(012)$        & 2\\
$(01)$           & 2 & $(012)(012)$   & 3\\
$(01)(02)$       & 3 & $(021)$        & 2\\
$(01)(12)$       & 3 & $(021)(021)$   & 3\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The maximum is $3=f(3)$, attained four times; $S(I_3)=(021)(021)$ is among the
attainers, as Lemma~\ref{lem:valid} promises. Note that a maximizer need not use large
steps --- $(01)(02)$, two transpositions, already attains it --- a first glimpse of the
all-size-2 phenomenon of Section~\ref{sec:outlook}.

\section{The certificate campaign}\label{sec:campaign}

The upper bound is discharged by SAT certificates over a formula defined in Lean. The formula
$\Phi_{49}$ (\texttt{SchedCNF6.schedCNFn 6 49} in \texttt{SchedCNF6.lean}; $84{,}882$ variables,
$2{,}709{,}212$ clauses) describes a legal schedule of Definition~\ref{def:sched} by a bounded
sequence of frames (the matching and visit masks after each step), derives the read-off
comparisons of Definition~\ref{def:readoff} from first visits, and asks for $49$ pairwise distinct
stable matchings of the read-off instance through selector slots in a fixed order. Satisfiability
of $\Phi_{49}$ therefore says: some legal schedule reads off to at least $49$ stable matchings.

\paragraph{Cubes.} $\Phi_{49}$ is refuted cube by cube. A cube fixes a step prefix by unit clauses
(\texttt{SchedCNF6.prefixUnits}); a \emph{closed} cube additionally pins the schedule to that
prefix (\texttt{Cubes6.stopUnits}). The root cubes are the $25{,}493$ first-appearance canonical
prefixes of length at most two (\texttt{Cubes6.canonicalCubes2}: the empty prefix closed, the $153$
length-one prefixes closed, and $25{,}339$ open length-two prefixes), which together cover every
legal schedule satisfying rule (a) of Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}; rule (b) is not used. A cube whose
solve exceeded a certificate-size or time watchdog was split into its canonical one-step extensions
(\texttt{Cubes6.extendCanon}) plus its closed copy: $1{,}804$ roots were split at depth two and
$952$ of their children at depth three, giving $321{,}492$ distinct cubes in all (by depth
$0/1/2/3/4$: $1$ / $153$ / $27{,}143$ / $197{,}814$ / $96{,}381$), of which $2{,}910$ are closed.
The $318{,}736$ leaves were each refuted by CaDiCaL~3.0.1 with LRAT output and each certificate
checked by the formally verified checker cake\_lpr~\cite{cakelpr}: $0$ satisfiable, $318{,}736$
\texttt{s VERIFIED UNSAT}. Solver time totalled $1{,}076{,}400$~s (median $1.0$~s per cube); the
certificates, about $25$~TB in all, were checked and deleted. The work ran on three machines
with one solver build throughout: a Linux/x86-64 container ($3{,}967$ leaves and $235$ splits,
checker built from the published x86-64 assembly \texttt{cake\_lpr.S}), an Apple M4~Max laptop
($12{,}727$ and $628$) and an Apple M4~Pro Mac mini ($302{,}042$ and $1{,}893$, checker built
from the published arm64 assembly \texttt{cake\_lpr\_arm8.S}).

\paragraph{Journal and audit.} Every run appends a record to a journal (cube id, depth, the
SHA-256 of the DIMACS file the solver read, the SHA-256 and size of the LRAT file, solver and
checker exit codes and output, timings). The audit (\texttt{cube\_campaign.py --audit}) rebuilds
the cube tree from the root generator and the recorded splits and checks that every leaf has a
consistent verified record: $25{,}493$ roots, $321{,}492$ nodes, $318{,}736$ verified, $0$ missing,
$0$ bad. The journal is self-attested: a ``verified'' record is the driver's transcription of
cake\_lpr's verdict, and the certificates no longer exist; independent verification of a verdict
means re-solving that cube, and the per-cube formula and certificate hashes let such a re-run be
compared with the journal record by record. The released journal
(\texttt{f6/campaign/campaign.jsonl.gz}, $40{,}943{,}262$ bytes) has SHA-256
\texttt{c9026b08045d8e8824c66d213bfa8eeb}\allowbreak\texttt{5336f118c22e030d42040136be7a8e4e}.

\paragraph{Identity with the Lean definitions.} What the solver saw was re-derived from Lean after
the campaign: the base formula printed by \texttt{export\_sched\_cnf} is byte-identical to the
driver's (SHA-256 \texttt{28421fb6\ldots}); the $25{,}493$ root cubes and the $295{,}999$ split
children printed by \texttt{export\_cubes6} are byte-identical to the driver's lists; the
Lean-defined certified cube set \texttt{Cubes6.finalCubes} (the roots refined by the $1{,}804$ and
$952$ recorded split ids, \texttt{SplitList6.lean}) equals the set of $318{,}736$ verified ids; and
all $321{,}492$ journaled formula hashes recompute from the Lean-printed header, base body and unit
clauses (\texttt{f6/lean\_rehash.py}). As a positive control against vacuity, the formula with the
dihedral schedule of Section~\ref{sec:extremal} pinned is satisfiable at $48$ slots and refuted at
$49$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:f6}
$f(6)=48$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The dihedral Latin instance attains $48$ (direct computation, three independent counters; OEIS
A351413). For the upper bound let $I$ be any instance. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:valid}
and~\ref{lem:bridge} (\texttt{validity\_unconditional}) there is a legal schedule $S$ with
$\mathrm{sc}(I)\le\mathrm{sc}(R(S))$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}(ii) $S$ may be taken to satisfy
rule (a). Such an $S$ fits one of the certified cubes, and if $\mathrm{sc}(R(S))\ge 49$ the
schedule and its read-off would define a satisfying assignment of that cube's formula. Every
certified cube's formula is unsatisfiable, so $\mathrm{sc}(I)\le 48$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is a Lean theorem]\label{rem:status}
Of the links in this proof, \texttt{validity\_unconditional} (Section~\ref{sec:lean}) is a Lean
theorem, the identity checks above tie the certified formulas to the Lean definitions, and the
certificates are checked by cake\_lpr. The remaining link is the faithfulness theorem: every
well-formed order-6 instance with at least $49$ stable matchings yields a satisfying assignment of
some certified cube's formula (the analogue of \texttt{cube\_faithful} in~\cite{f5paper}), which
packages the schedule-level relabelling of Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}(ii), the coverage of canonical
schedules by \texttt{finalCubes}, and the satisfaction of every clause family by the natural
assignment. Its status is recorded in the repository's \texttt{STATUS.md} at the commit cited in
Section~\ref{sec:outlook}.

\section{The enumeration (corroboration)}\label{sec:enum}

Independently of the certificates, an exhaustive enumeration evaluates every schedule node.
The enumerator (\texttt{gen\_enum.c}, $\sim$200 lines of C) performs a depth-first search over
schedules. The steps available at order $6$ are the
\[
\sum_{k=2}^{6}\binom{6}{k}(k-1)!\;=\;15+40+90+144+120\;=\;409
\]
cyclic moves on $2..6$ men in min-first form; state comprises the current matching,
per-person trajectories and visit masks; the reductions of Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}(a,b) and
the caps of Definition~\ref{def:sched} prune the tree. At \emph{every} node the read-off
instance is built (trajectory prefix + bottom completion) and its stable-matching count
computed exactly by scanning all $720$ perfect matchings. Sharding on the depth-1 step
splits the work across cores. The same machine, parametrized by order, produces:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
order & step shapes & nodes & $\max_S \mathrm{sc}(R(S))$ & $f(n)$\\
\midrule
3 & 5   & 8                    & 3  & 3\\
4 & 20  & 409                  & 10 & 10\\
5 & 84  & 498{,}599            & 16 & 16\\
6 & 409 & 26{,}574{,}282{,}886 & 48 & \textbf{48}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The full run ($512$ shards, $8$ cores of an Apple M4 Max, $\sim$10 hours) visits
$26{,}574{,}282{,}886$ nodes, evaluates all of them, and attains
\[
\max_S\ \mathrm{sc}(R(S)) \;=\; 48 .
\]
Because every node is evaluated, no monotonicity assumption about schedule extension is needed:
for any instance $I$, the node $S(I)$ itself is evaluated (Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}). This reproves the
upper bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:f6} by a route that shares no code with the campaign of
Section~\ref{sec:campaign}; its trust base is the C program and both halves of
Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}, neither of which is machine-checked, so we present it as corroboration of the
certificates rather than as the proof.


\section{The extremal instance}\label{sec:extremal}

The unique-up-to-symmetry maximizer known at order $6$ is Eilers' Latin instance built
from the dihedral group (OEIS A351413):
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
m_0 & w_0 & w_1 & w_2 & w_3 & w_4 & w_5\\
m_1 & w_1 & w_0 & w_3 & w_2 & w_5 & w_4\\
m_2 & w_4 & w_3 & w_0 & w_5 & w_2 & w_1\\
m_3 & w_3 & w_4 & w_5 & w_0 & w_1 & w_2\\
m_4 & w_2 & w_5 & w_4 & w_1 & w_0 & w_3\\
m_5 & w_5 & w_2 & w_1 & w_4 & w_3 & w_0
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
w_0 & m_5 & m_4 & m_3 & m_2 & m_1 & m_0\\
w_1 & m_2 & m_3 & m_4 & m_5 & m_0 & m_1\\
w_2 & m_3 & m_2 & m_1 & m_0 & m_5 & m_4\\
w_3 & m_4 & m_5 & m_0 & m_1 & m_2 & m_3\\
w_4 & m_1 & m_0 & m_5 & m_4 & m_3 & m_2\\
w_5 & m_0 & m_1 & m_2 & m_3 & m_4 & m_5
\end{array}
\]
(rows list partners from most to least preferred; preferences are fully opposed:
woman $w$ ranks man $m$ in position $5-r$ whenever $m$ ranks $w$ in position $r$). Its structural profile is extreme in every measured respect: it has
\emph{fifteen} rotations, every one a transposition (size $2$), for a total move count
of $30$ --- the full budget $n(n-1)$ of Fact~\ref{fact:budget} with every man moving the
maximal five times, i.e.\ every man's trajectory is his entire preference list. The
rotation poset on these fifteen transpositions has $24$ cover relations and exactly $48$
downsets, which our three independent counters confirm as its stable-matching count.
In the enumeration, only the shard family containing this schedule reaches $48$;
regions of the tree using any rotation of size $\ge 3$ peak strictly lower.

\section{Validation}\label{sec:valid}

\paragraph{Ground truth.} The same enumerator, parametrized by order, reproduces every known
value: order $3$ ($8$ nodes, max $3$), order $4$ ($409$ nodes, max $10$), order $5$
($498{,}599$ nodes, max $16$). The last is decisive: $f(5)=16$ is certified in the companion
development~\cite{f5paper} by a Lean~4 proof with SAT refutations checked end-to-end by the
formally verified checker cake\_lpr --- a machine-checked theorem serving as test oracle for the
present method.

\paragraph{Symmetry on/off.} Disabling both reductions of Lemma~\ref{lem:sym} multiplies the tree
($4.5\times$ at order 4; $14\times$ at order 5; $48\times$ in a budget-16 order-6 check of
$2{,}657{,}773{,}436$ nodes) and never changes a maximum.

\paragraph{Independent implementation.} A separately written Python enumerator (different pruning,
different code path) agrees on the maxima at the budgets where it is feasible; a third,
$40$-line reference implementation written directly from
Definition~\ref{def:sched} and Lemma~\ref{lem:sym} reproduces the order-3 table of
Section~\ref{sec:example} node for node. A fourth route bypasses enumeration entirely: a
SAT reformulation of the schedule space (bounded frames, first-visit-derived read-off
comparisons, selector slots) refutes ``some order-5 schedule has $\mathrm{sc}(R(S))\ge
17$'' in four minutes, with the refutation checked end-to-end by \textsf{drat-trim} and
\textsf{cake\_lpr} --- both an independent certificate-backed confirmation at order $5$
and the pilot for the order-6 campaign of Section~\ref{sec:campaign} (design notes in
\texttt{REPLAY\_DESIGN.md} in the repository).

\paragraph{Other search spaces.} Before the enumeration, $9.7\times10^7$ hill-climbing
evaluations in raw instance space and $1.2\times10^9$ evaluations in swap-schedule space (72
million valid schedules) never exceeded $48$; an earlier maximal-only pass of the full enumeration
($1.40\times10^{10}$ exact counts) also attained $48$.

\paragraph{Structural corroboration.} All $450$ sampled order-5 maximizers share a single
rotation-poset skeleton; the dihedral order-6 instance uses the full budget of fifteen size-2
rotations; and across the enumeration only the shard containing the dihedral family reaches $48$,
with large-rotation shards peaking strictly lower --- consistent with extremal instances being
full-budget, all-size-2 phenomena.


\section{Formalization in Lean 4}\label{sec:lean}

Lemmas~\ref{lem:valid} and~\ref{lem:bridge} are formalized in Lean~4 with Mathlib in
the accompanying development. The reduction layer is ten files in import order from
\texttt{SixBridge.lean} through \texttt{WRelabel6.lean} ($4{,}646$ lines); together with
\texttt{SchedCNF6.lean}, which defines the campaign formula $\Phi_{49}$ as a Lean value, it is
$5{,}230$ lines and $243$ theorems --- the figure quoted elsewhere in this paper and in the
repository --- atop the order-5 infrastructure of~\cite{f5paper}. The end statement is
unconditional: for every well-formed order-6
instance $I$ there is a legal schedule $S$ with $\mathrm{sc}(I)\le\mathrm{sc}(R(S))$
(\texttt{validity\_unconditional}), obtained by composing the validity chain with a
\emph{bottom-agnostic} form of the bridge (\texttt{count\_le\_of\_orderPreserving}:
$J$ dominates $I$ whenever $J$ ranks each person's $I$-stable partners on top in
$I$-order, with no condition on the rest of the lists) and a women-only relabelling that
normalizes the man-optimal matching to the identity
(\texttt{stableCount6\_wrelabel6}, \texttt{manOpt\_wrelabel6}). Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}
is not formalized in full: its relabelling half (ii) is proved at the instance level
(\texttt{Sym6.lean}), and the schedule-level form used by Theorem~\ref{thm:f6} is part of the
faithfulness theorem whose status is recorded in \texttt{STATUS.md} (Remark~\ref{rem:status});
its commutation half (i) is validated computationally only (canonicalization on/off agreement) and
is used by the enumeration alone. The certificate campaign of Section~\ref{sec:campaign} needs
the relabelling half (ii) --- its root cubes cover only first-appearance canonical schedules ---
and not the commutation half (i). Every theorem is proved with no \texttt{sorry}, and
\texttt{\#print axioms} reports only the standard \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice},
\texttt{Quot.sound}; continuous integration (on pushes to \texttt{main}, pull requests and manual
dispatch) rebuilds the development from source with the standard Mathlib olean cache and re-checks
the no-\texttt{sorry} and axiom conditions.

\paragraph{Design.} Instances are pairs of $6\times 6$ rank tables over
\texttt{List Nat}; a matching is a list, stability is a decidable predicate, and the
set of stable matchings \texttt{sms6 I} is computed by filtering the (structurally
recursive, hence kernel-computable) permutation enumeration. The formalization uses
\emph{no} Gale--Shapley algorithm and \emph{no} rotation machinery: rotations, which
carry the classical proofs, never appear. This is a deliberate trade --- the
classical route (rotations exist, form a poset, covers eliminate single rotations)
is textbook material but formalization-heavy, whereas everything the reduction
actually needs can be rebuilt from elementary list lemmas.

\paragraph{The bridge lemma.} The read-off instance is defined by a promotion
operator: \texttt{readoff I} moves each person's stable partners (a decidable
predicate) to the top of their list, preserving relative order. The Lean statement
of Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge} is a direct case analysis: a would-be blocking pair of a
stable matching in the read-off instance either involves a non-trajectory partner
(ranked below everything relevant by promotion) or maps back to a blocking pair of
the original instance.

\paragraph{The chain.} Lemma~\ref{lem:valid} needs the lattice traversal. Instead
of rotation elimination, the development constructs the maximal chain directly:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
def chainFrom (I : Inst6) : Nat $\to$ List Nat $\to$ List (List Nat)\\
\phantom{xx}| 0, cur => [cur]\\
\phantom{xx}| fuel+1, cur => match (sms6 I).filter (strictDomB I cur) with\\
\phantom{xxxx}| [] => [cur]\\
\phantom{xxxx}| c :: cs => cur :: chainFrom I fuel (pickMin I c cs)
\end{quote}
where \texttt{pickMin} selects, among the strict dominators of the current
matching, one of \emph{minimum total rank-sum}. This argmin choice does the work of
rotation theory for free: any stable matching strictly between the current one and
its successor would have a strictly smaller rank-sum than the successor, so every
step is automatically a \emph{cover} of the dominance order; the rank-sum strictly
increases and is bounded by $30$, so fuel $31$ provably suffices and the chain ends
at the woman-optimal matching. Completeness --- every stable pair of $I$ appears on
the chain --- follows from a \emph{no-skip} lemma (on a cover $\mu\lessdot\nu$, no
man passes over a stable partner) by squeezing each stable pair's rank between the
chain's endpoints. The endpoint matchings themselves are obtained lattice-style:
the meet of two stable matchings is stable (Knuth's lemma, proved by blocking-pair
extraction over lists), a fold of meets gives the man-optimal matching, and the
woman-optimal side comes free by a transposition functor that swaps the two sides
and inverts matchings.

\paragraph{Trajectories, budgets, cycles.} Each clause of
Definition~\ref{def:sched} and Lemma~\ref{lem:valid} is an individual theorem about
the chain:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
clause & Lean theorem(s)\\
\midrule
runs from man-optimal to woman-optimal & \texttt{chainFrom\_head}, \texttt{chainFrom\_last}\\
all elements stable matchings & \texttt{chainFrom\_mem}, \texttt{chain\_mem\_perm}\\
consecutive elements are covers & \texttt{chainFrom\_cover}\\
every stable pair realized & \texttt{chain\_complete}\\
men descend their lists, exactly the stable partners & \texttt{traj\_mem\_iff}, \texttt{traj\_rank\_pairwise}\\
from optimal to pessimal partner & \texttt{traj\_head}, \texttt{traj\_getLast}\\
no revisits, either side & \texttt{traj\_nodup}, \texttt{wtraj\_nodup}\\
$\le 5$ moves per man, $\le 30$ total & \texttt{traj\_length\_le}, \texttt{total\_moves\_le\_30}\\
steps are disjoint cyclic swaps, size $\ge 2$ & \texttt{prevOwner\_moved/ne/inj/getD}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The last row deserves a remark: the classical statement ``every cover eliminates a
single rotation'' is \emph{not} formalized, because it is not needed. For coverage
of the schedule space it suffices that each step's moved set decomposes into
disjoint cycles of size $\ge 2$ (the map ``previous owner of my new wife'' is a
fixed-point-free injective self-map of the moved set --- four short permutation
lemmas); a multi-cycle step splits into consecutive single-cycle steps with
identical trajectories.

\paragraph{What remains.} The Lean-side identity checks of Section~\ref{sec:campaign} tie
the base formula, the cube lists, the certified cube set and every cube formula to the Lean
definitions. The link not covered by the theorems above is faithfulness, in the direction the
bound needs: every well-formed order-6 instance with at least $49$ stable matchings yields a
satisfying assignment of some certified cube's formula (instance to model; the analogue of
\texttt{cube\_faithful} for $f(5)$). Its proof consists of the schedule-level relabelling of
Lemma~\ref{lem:sym}(ii), the coverage of first-appearance canonical schedules by
\texttt{finalCubes}, and satisfaction lemmas for each clause family under the natural assignment.
Its status is recorded in the repository's \texttt{STATUS.md} at the commit cited in
Section~\ref{sec:outlook}.

\section{Related work and outlook}\label{sec:outlook}

The computational lineage on $f(n)$ is Eilers' ($f(5)$, the uniqueness observation at $f(4)$,
and the extremal-profile counts, which we replicated independently in~\cite{f5paper}), building on
Thurber~\cite{thurber} and Knuth~\cite{knuth}; the asymptotic bounds are \cite{kow,pp}. Certified
computational values of comparable flavour include $R(4,5)=25$, the empty hexagon number, Keller's
conjecture and Schur number five; our companion paper~\cite{f5paper} follows that tradition for
$f(5)$, and Sections~\ref{sec:lean} and~\ref{sec:campaign} carry it to the order-6 bound: the
reduction is a Lean theorem, the certificates are checked by cake\_lpr, and the status of the
faithfulness theorem is recorded in \texttt{STATUS.md} (Remark~\ref{rem:status}). The next open
value, $f(7)$ (budget $42$), had published lower bound $81$ (Ong, Ang, Ho, Eilers, Marks and
Buzi~\cite{ong24}, improving Thurber's $71$~\cite{thurber}); the accompanying repository raises it
to $85$ (\texttt{f7/lb85.txt}: two all-transposition schedules of length $20$ found by local
search). Certifying $f(7)$ appears to need either a much faster certified evaluator or new
structural cuts: the schedule tree grows by three to four orders of magnitude.

\paragraph{An open problem.} The extremal data of
Sections~\ref{sec:example} and~\ref{sec:extremal} share a pattern. At orders $3$
through $6$ the maximum is attained by an \emph{all-transposition} schedule (every
step of size $2$): $(01)(02)$ at order $3$; six transpositions (the full budget
$12$) at order $4$; eight transpositions ($16$ of the budget $20$) at order $5$;
fifteen transpositions (the full budget $30$) at order $6$. At the \emph{even}
orders the extremal schedule exhausts the budget --- every man's trajectory is his
entire list --- while at the odd orders $3$ and $5$ it does not: at $n=3$ no legal full-budget
all-transposition schedule exists, and at $n=5$ every legal full-budget all-transposition
schedule reads off to at most $11<16$ (by enumeration under
Definitions~\ref{def:sched}--\ref{def:readoff}). Whether the extremal schedule is sub-budget at
every odd order is part of the observation, not a theorem. This is consistent with Eilers'
observation that the natural lower-bound constructions look exact only at even orders.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:size2}
For every $n\ge 3$, $f(n)$ is attained by the read-off of an all-size-2 schedule;
for even $n$, by a full-budget one ($n(n-1)/2$ transposition steps).
\end{conjecture}

If true, computing $f(n)$ reduces to an optimization over sequences of
transpositions --- sorting-network-like objects --- a space of roughly
$(n(n-1)/2)!^{1/2}$ rather than $n!^{2n}$, and structural insight into it could
sharpen the $2.28^n$--$3.55^n$ asymptotic band.
